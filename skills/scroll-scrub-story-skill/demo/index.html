The High North

Twelve guests, three camps and a coastline that has no road to it. We go where the ice decides, and we come back with less noise in our heads.

Every season we read the sea ice with the same people who have read it for twenty years. Nothing on the schedule is more important than that conversation.

Ingrid Solvang Founder and expedition lead

Hornsund, late May. Light around the clock and the fjord still frozen at the head.

Our camps

Three seasonal camps, each a day apart by boat. Heated, quiet, and taken down without a trace in September.

Lysning

Our first camp, on a raised beach below the glacier front. Eight domes, one long table, and the sound of ice calving at night.

[ 77° 00′ 12″ N, 15° 33′ 40″ E ]

Drift

Built for the far north coast. Glass-roofed sleeping pods that face the pack ice, with a sauna on the shingle.

[ 79° 51′ 05″ N, 18° 12′ 30″ E ]

Nordlys

Our late-season camp, open into the first dark weeks. Wood-lined huts, a heated lookout, and the aurora from your bed.

[ 78° 13′ 41″ N, 13° 49′ 02″ E ]
